# Riyadh–Colombo Market Evidence

## Preliminary Route Assessment

Riyadh–Colombo is not currently an unserved market. SriLankan Airlines already operates nonstop flights between the two cities.

The project will therefore evaluate whether a hypothetical Saudi low-cost carrier could enter the route, stimulate additional demand and operate profitably alongside the incumbent airline.

## Verified Route Facts

| Factor | Current evidence |
|---|---|
| Origin | Riyadh King Khalid International Airport (RUH) |
| Destination | Bandaranaike International Airport, Colombo (CMB) |
| Approximate distance | 4,040 kilometres / 2,182 nautical miles |
| Estimated nonstop flight time | Approximately 5–5.5 hours |
| Existing nonstop airline | SriLankan Airlines |
| Indicated nonstop frequency | Approximately 7 flights per week |
| Potential aircraft | Airbus A320neo |
| A320neo published range | Approximately 3,400 nautical miles |
| Saudi Arabia–Sri Lanka trade | Approximately USD 300 million in combined 2025 trade |

The route is within the published range of an Airbus A320neo. However, real operational feasibility would also depend on payload, weather, fuel reserves, baggage volume and airport conditions.

## Initial Demand Segments

### 1. Visiting Friends and Relatives

Sri Lankan residents working in Saudi Arabia are likely to form an important part of the addressable market.

This segment is expected to be:

- Price-sensitive
- Relatively resilient
- More likely to purchase checked baggage
- Influenced by school holidays, religious holidays and employment cycles

The exact size of the Riyadh catchment still needs to be researched.

### 2. Leisure Travel

Tourism demand between Saudi Arabia and Sri Lanka currently appears relatively modest.

Sri Lanka recorded approximately 1,757 arrivals from Saudi Arabia during the first half of 2026, compared with 2,555 during the equivalent period in 2025.

This suggests that leisure demand alone may not be sufficient to support the route. Competitive fares, destination marketing and holiday packages may be required to stimulate demand.

### 3. Business and Government Travel

Business demand is likely to be smaller than the visiting-friends-and-relatives segment, but growing trade links may provide some higher-value passenger and cargo demand.

Sri Lankan exports to Saudi Arabia reached approximately USD 137.85 million in 2025, while imports from Saudi Arabia reached approximately USD 161.85 million.

### 4. Connecting Passengers

Colombo could provide onward connectivity to parts of South Asia and Southeast Asia.

However, the value of this segment would depend on airline partnerships, schedule coordination and through-ticketing capabilities.

## Initial Strategic Implications

- The airline would compete against an established nonstop operator.
- Price and schedule convenience would be central to the proposition.
- Checked baggage could be an important ancillary product.
- A three- or four-weekly launch may carry less risk than immediately operating daily.
- Demand stimulation would probably be required.
- Tourism demand should not be treated as the sole justification for launching the route.
- Directional demand and revenue may be uneven.
- Airport incentives and cargo revenue could materially affect profitability.

## Working Hypotheses

The following are hypotheses to test rather than confirmed facts:

1. Visiting-friends-and-relatives traffic will form the route’s largest segment.
2. Customers will accept a low-cost product if baggage options remain competitive.
3. Three or four weekly flights could achieve a higher initial load factor than daily service.
4. Evening or overnight timings may suit passenger preferences and aircraft utilisation.
5. The route may require a lower break-even load factor or additional cargo revenue to remain commercially attractive.

## Information Still Required
